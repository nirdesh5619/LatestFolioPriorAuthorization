I wanted to share a brief overview of the Prior Authorization (PA) Multi-Agent Platform I’ve been building. It is a demonstration system designed to show how AI can make the prior authorization process faster, more transparent, and more consistent, while keeping a human clinician firmly in control of every decision.

**The Problem**

Prior authorization can be a slow and frustrating part of healthcare administration. A provider submits a request for a procedure, medication, or medical equipment, and the payer must evaluate it against detailed coverage criteria such as BMI thresholds, prior treatments, documentation requirements, and exclusions.

In many workflows, this process can be manual and difficult to understand. A patient or provider may receive a denial without a clear explanation of which criteria were not met or what additional evidence could change the outcome.

**What the Platform Does Differently**

The platform evaluates every coverage criterion individually and shows the specific patient evidence supporting each criterion. Rather than relying on a black-box AI decision, the actual approve, deny, or pend determination is made by a deterministic rules engine. This makes the outcome consistent, explainable, and auditable.

**Core Components**

* **Frontend (React):** Patient records, request submission, live AI pipeline visualization, clinician review queue, and operations dashboard.
* **Backend (FastAPI/Python):** REST API that runs the six-agent evaluation pipeline and streams progress in real time.
* **Retrieval Layer (RAG):** Vector search across the payer policy library to identify the applicable policy document for each request.
* **LLM Integration (Anthropic/OpenAI):** Used to generate a plain-language explanation of a decision that has already been determined by the rules engine, rather than making the decision itself.
* **Human-in-the-Loop Review:** No determination is final until a clinical reviewer signs off. Reviewers can uphold or override the recommendation, with an explanation required for an override.
* **Observability Dashboard:** System health, request volume, outcomes, reviewer override rates, and AI costs, with time-based filtering.
* **Audit Trail & Reporting:** Every pipeline step, criterion evaluation, and review decision is recorded and can be exported as a PDF report.

**How the Multi-Agent Approach Helps**

The request moves through six specialized agents in sequence: **intake, guideline retrieval, risk assessment, criteria matching, determination, and safety validation.** Each agent has a focused responsibility rather than relying on one system to perform the entire workflow.

This provides several benefits:

* **Faster answers:** The pipeline can process a request in seconds and provide live visibility into its progress.
* **Clearer reasons:** Each criterion shows whether it was met, not met, or requires additional information, along with the supporting evidence.
* **Fewer inappropriate denials:** Borderline cases can be flagged as needing more information rather than relying on an uncertain automated denial.
* **Built-in safety checks:** A dedicated safety agent independently cross-checks the evaluation and can flag potential safety concerns, such as a documented allergy overlapping with a current medication.
* **Human oversight:** Every AI-assisted determination, including approvals, is routed to a clinical reviewer before becoming final.

The platform is currently a **demonstration system using entirely synthetic patients and synthetic payer policies**. It is not connected to any real payer, EHR, or claims system. Its purpose is to demonstrate what a more transparent, evidence-backed prior authorization workflow could look like from end to end.
