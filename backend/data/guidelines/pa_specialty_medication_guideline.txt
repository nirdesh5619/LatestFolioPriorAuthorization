GUIDELINE: DEMO PRIOR AUTHORIZATION CRITERIA - SPECIALTY MEDICATIONS (BIOLOGICS)
VERSION: 1.0
ORGANIZATION: Synthetic Payer Clinical Policy Unit
STATUS: DEMONSTRATION DATA - NOT AN ACTUAL PAYER POLICY

SECTION: Coverage Criteria

Criterion 1: A specialty biologic medication is considered medically
necessary when the patient has a confirmed diagnosis consistent with the
medication's indication, such as rheumatoid arthritis, psoriatic arthritis,
inflammatory bowel disease, or moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis.

Criterion 2: The patient must have documented an inadequate response,
intolerance, or contraindication to at least one conventional first-line
therapy (such as methotrexate or another conventional disease-modifying
agent) before a biologic is authorized, consistent with step therapy.

Criterion 3: Baseline screening for latent tuberculosis and hepatitis B
must be documented prior to initiating a biologic agent, given the
infection risk associated with this drug class.

Criterion 4: For continuation of therapy beyond the initial authorization
period, documentation of clinical response (such as reduced disease
activity score or improved symptom control) is required.

SECTION: Step Therapy and Conservative Treatment Requirements

At least one conventional (non-biologic) systemic therapy must be tried and
documented as inadequate, not tolerated, or contraindicated before a
biologic is approved, per Criterion 2.

SECTION: Documentation Requirements

Requests must include the confirmed diagnosis, prior conventional therapies
tried with duration and outcome, baseline infection screening results, and
the prescribing specialist's assessment.

SECTION: Exclusions

Use of a biologic medication for an indication not supported by the
confirmed diagnosis, or without documented step therapy, is not considered
medically necessary under this policy.

SECTION: Safety

Determinations under this policy should consider infection risk, documented
allergies to the specific biologic or its components, and any current
medications with known interactions. Any determination generated from this
policy must be reviewed by a qualified clinical reviewer before
communication to the patient or provider.
