GUIDELINE: DEMO PRIOR AUTHORIZATION CRITERIA - BARIATRIC SURGERY
VERSION: 1.0
ORGANIZATION: Synthetic Payer Clinical Policy Unit
STATUS: DEMONSTRATION DATA - NOT AN ACTUAL PAYER POLICY

SECTION: Coverage Criteria

Criterion 1: Bariatric surgery is considered medically necessary for adults
with a body mass index (BMI) of 40 or greater, regardless of comorbidities.

Criterion 2: Bariatric surgery is considered medically necessary for adults
with a BMI of 35 to 39.9 when at least one obesity-related comorbidity is
documented, such as type 2 diabetes, hypertension, obstructive sleep apnea,
or dyslipidemia.

Criterion 3: The patient must have documented participation in a supervised
weight-management program for at least six months within the two years
preceding the request, including nutrition counseling.

Criterion 4: A psychological evaluation confirming the patient is an
appropriate candidate for bariatric surgery, without unmanaged conditions
that would impair postoperative adherence, is required prior to approval.

Criterion 5: The patient must not have a documented contraindication to
surgery, such as an unstable cardiopulmonary condition or active substance
use disorder, that has not been addressed.

SECTION: Step Therapy and Conservative Treatment Requirements

Non-surgical weight-management approaches, including supervised diet and
exercise programs, must be attempted and documented before bariatric surgery
is authorized, consistent with Criterion 3.

SECTION: Documentation Requirements

Requests must include current BMI, weight and height, documented
obesity-related comorbidities, evidence of the supervised weight-management
program, and the psychological evaluation outcome.

SECTION: Exclusions

Bariatric surgery requested primarily for cosmetic reasons without a
qualifying BMI and comorbidity profile is not considered medically
necessary under this policy.

SECTION: Safety

Determinations under this policy should consider cardiovascular risk,
diabetes control, and any documented allergies or medication interactions
relevant to perioperative management. Any determination generated from this
policy must be reviewed by a qualified clinical reviewer before
communication to the patient or provider.
