GUIDELINE: DEMO PRIOR AUTHORIZATION CRITERIA - DURABLE MEDICAL EQUIPMENT (DME)
VERSION: 1.0
ORGANIZATION: Synthetic Payer Clinical Policy Unit
STATUS: DEMONSTRATION DATA - NOT AN ACTUAL PAYER POLICY

SECTION: Coverage Criteria

Criterion 1: A power wheelchair is considered medically necessary when the
patient has a documented mobility limitation that prevents safe completion
of activities of daily living within the home, and a manual wheelchair or
scooter is documented as insufficient.

Criterion 2: A hospital bed is considered medically necessary when the
patient has a documented medical condition requiring specific body
positioning not achievable with an ordinary bed, such as significant
respiratory compromise or a need for frequent position changes.

Criterion 3: Continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) equipment is
considered medically necessary following a diagnostic sleep study
confirming moderate or severe obstructive sleep apnea.

Criterion 4: A face-to-face evaluation by the ordering provider documenting
the functional need for the requested equipment within the preceding six
months is required for all durable medical equipment requests.

SECTION: Step Therapy and Conservative Treatment Requirements

For mobility equipment, a lower level of equipment (such as a cane, walker,
or manual wheelchair) should be documented as trialed or considered
insufficient before a power mobility device is authorized.

SECTION: Documentation Requirements

Requests must include the qualifying diagnosis, the specific functional
limitation, prior equipment tried, and the date and findings of the
required face-to-face evaluation.

SECTION: Exclusions

Equipment requested primarily for caregiver convenience without a
documented patient functional limitation is not considered medically
necessary under this policy.

SECTION: Safety

Determinations under this policy should consider home environment
suitability, caregiver support, and any safety risks associated with the
requested equipment. Any determination generated from this policy must be
reviewed by a qualified clinical reviewer before communication to the
patient or provider.
